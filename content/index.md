**This site is to document the connections between the major books related to Judaism (J), Christianity (C), and Gnosticism (G), and those influenced majorly by such.
Characters, places, etc, also have their own pages that link within the books.**

# INFO

## WHEN?
Started September 9th, 2026. 

## WHO?
Made by an American agnostic.

## WHY?
Multiple reasons:

a. I wanted to reread religious texts, starting with the Bible to see my newer thoughts on them. 

b. Boredom, doing these links in notes whilst reading made me more engaged with the content of the books

c. Most sites that document this type of thing tend to leave out many entries of insignificant people, my goal is to include every single unique instance of a person/place I can if it is able to be described at all.

## HOW?
The text used for the verses in the books of the biblical canon, uses the NRSVue (New Revised Standard Version, updated edition), so there's some variations of spellings not used elsewhere, and this may affect note count, and may explain why certain spellings of a name you are unable to find on this site.

Of course there are Christians who prefer the KJV, NASB, NIV, ESV, RSV, and so on and on, but since I don't have any specific interpretational lens I am actively trying to apply to the scriptures (such as literalism), I don't have preference for these texts and the NRSVue is generally approved of.

Every time a person or place or something else relevant is mentioned within a place in these books/writings, I link it, so overtime it shows connections between where these people/places are mentioned and who/what they are.

There is only one note for a given name/place, if multiple people/places fall under the same exact name, it is clarified with a list of those things, for example "Can refer to:"

For Jewish, Christian, and major Gnostic texts that are not of the Biblical canon, the most scholarly or best understood English translation is to be used.

If texts have too much missing material, to the point of unable to be understood in any great capacity, if once known/influential, they will likely be excluded.

Only one instance of a given person/place can be linked per chapter, for example if the same person is named twice in the same chapter, only the first occurrence of it is highlighted, this may be arbitrary but it is less about "counts" of how many times  a person/location is mentioned, but this is to show the times a place/location is mentioned in unique contexts and stories, and this seems to be better. This also saves me time from having to link every single mention of a thing.

The singular version of groups such as just "Israelite", "Levite", etc, are not linking to anything as it would inflate note count when the plural version is used about as commonly so and is sufficient. This also applies to descriptions of groups, orders, descendants, or other texts, etc, such as "Aaronic" and "levitical", or I may just highlight certain things within those singular/plural versions, for example the Aaron in Aaronic is linked.

Within the notes of people/places, explanations of them will often link to other topics, not all notes link to chapters, this inflates connections a bit depending what you deem a connection, so for example Peter will lead to Linus who is mentioned in the scriptures very briefly, but then after Linus the next Pope, and so on to current Pope, because each is relevant because the succession is relevant when mentioning the papacy at all beginning with Peter.

Locations and people that are highly popular or well documented or known outside of the Bible (excluding Jesus, Moses, and the apostles for example) do not link to anywhere as it would show up too much whilst linking to too much, such as Israel, Jerusalem, Galilee, this may be weirdest/most controversial "rule" but this is something that'd be too much of a pain to make a note for and go into and frankly part of my goal here of this project is to learn more about the less known and allow others to do similiar.

Phrases or relevant titles or designations that go before certain people/locations may/often are not included in the title of a link for that person/location, for example "Kingdom of Judah" isn't its own link, but the "Judah" in that title, would be a link.
