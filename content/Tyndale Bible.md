[[William Tyndale]]’s version is considered the first English translation of the Hebrew and Greek texts, and the first to be mass-produced. He did not complete the whole Bible during his lifetime. His New Testament was published in 1525, and the Old Testament in 1530. In response to his writings, Tyndale was strangled for the charge of heresy by the Roman Catholic church. Among his crimes: maintaining the biblical teaching that faith alone justifies a man before God. [[Myles Coverdale]] finished Tyndale’s work about 2 years after his execution. According to recent scholarship, 75% of the [[King James Version]]'s Old Testament and 84% of the New Testament comes directly from Tyndale’s translation.
