Born June 28th, 1703, died March 2nd, 1791. English cleric, theologian, and evangelist who was a principal leader of a revival movement within the Church of England known as Methodism. The societies he founded became the dominant form of the ongoing independent Methodist movement. Educated at Charterhouse and Christ Church, Oxford, Wesley was elected a fellow of Lincoln College, Oxford, in 1726 and ordained as an Anglican priest two years later. Brother of [[Charles Wesley]] and friend and colleague of [[George Whitefield]]. Wesley rejected predestination and the theology of [[John Calvin]].
